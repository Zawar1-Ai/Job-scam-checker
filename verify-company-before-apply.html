How to Verify a Company Before You Apply

Verifying a company takes a few minutes, and it is the strongest habit a beginner job seeker can build. Scammers copy real company names, build convincing fake sites, and use friendly recruiters to make a listing look real. The checks below do not rely on what the recruiter sent you. They rely on what you can find independently.

1. Find the job on the official careers page

Search the company name and the job title yourself, then open the company's own website and careers page. Type the address yourself instead of tapping a link in a message or email. If the job is not listed there, and the recruiter cannot explain why, treat that as a warning sign. Small companies may post only on job boards, but they should still have a real website, contact details, and a way to reach them outside the recruiter's chat.

2. Check the recruiter's domain

Look at the email domain, not just the name. A real recruiter usually emails from the company domain, for example name@company.com, not from a free personal account. If the whole process stays on WhatsApp or Telegram, with no company email and no careers page, slow down. You can also contact the company through the contact details on its official site and ask whether the role and the recruiter are real.

3. Search the company name plus scam and reviews

Search the company name with the words scam and reviews, and read more than one result. Look for patterns: other people reporting the same message, the same fee, or the same cheque trick. One bad review does not prove a scam, but repeated reports of fees, instant offers, or money-moving work should stop you. Also watch for names that are almost identical to a well-known brand, with a small spelling change.

4. Check the interview and the pay

Real hiring usually includes at least a short interview or skills test. An instant offer with no questions is a warning sign. Compare the pay with similar roles. Pay far above normal for simple work with no experience is designed to make you stop checking. If the pay, hours, and daily duties are vague, ask for them in writing through an official channel before you proceed.

5. Check the money and details rules

A real employer never asks you to pay to start, deposit a cheque and send money onward, or share your Social Security number, bank login, or ID photo over chat before a verified interview. Personal details should come late in the process, through an official hiring system, not in a first message.

Quick verification checklist

  • Job found on the official careers page, or the company explains clearly why not.
  • Recruiter reachable from a company domain, not only a chat app.
  • No upfront payment of any kind.
  • Real interview or skills test, not an instant offer.
  • Pay in line with similar roles, and clear daily work.
  • Personal details requested only late, through an official system.

If a listing fails two or more checks, skip it. There are many real listings; losing one suspicious one costs you nothing.
