Fake Cheque Job Scam Explained

The fake cheque scam is one of the most costly job scams for beginners, because it looks like you are already being paid. A supposed employer sends you a cheque, often for more than you expected, and asks you to deposit it, keep part of it as your pay, and send the rest onward to buy equipment, pay a vendor, or cover a fee. The cheque later bounces, but the money you sent is already gone, and your bank may hold you responsible for it.

How it usually works, step by step

  1. You are hired quickly, often with no real interview, for a remote role such as a personal assistant, mystery shopper, or package handler.
  2. A cheque arrives, sometimes by mail or as a mobile deposit image, for equipment, supplies, or your first pay.
  3. You are told to deposit it fast, keep your share, and send the rest by bank transfer, gift card, or another payment method to a third party.
  4. The bank may initially show the money as available, which makes the cheque look real. Days later it bounces as fake, stolen, or unfunded.
  5. You are left owing the bank for the full amount you sent onward, plus any fees.

The simple rule

A real employer pays you. It does not route payments through you to a third party, and it does not ask you to buy equipment from its vendor with a cheque it just sent you. If a job involves depositing a cheque and sending money onward, stop.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Instant offer, no interview, and a cheque before any real work.
  • Pressure to deposit and send money the same day.
  • Payment by gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfer, or a personal account, instead of normal payroll.
  • Vague job duties, a recruiter only on chat apps, and no verifiable careers page listing.

What to do if you received a cheque

Do not deposit it, and do not send money onward. Contact your bank before spending any of it, and ask the bank to check the cheque. Save the job post, messages, and the cheque details, and report the listing on the platform where you found it. If you already sent money, contact your bank immediately and report the scam to the FTC fraud reporting site in the USA.
