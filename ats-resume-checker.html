        What is an ATS, and why does it matter?

        An Applicant Tracking System is software that stores and ranks applications before a recruiter sees them. When 200 people apply for one remote job, nobody reads 200 resumes — the ATS searches them for the words in the job description, checks that it can read the file at all, and shows the recruiter a shortlist.

        If your resume doesn't contain the words the employer used, you can be fully qualified and still never be seen. That's fixable, and it's exactly what the keyword match, section breakdown, and risk list above show you.

        The 6 mistakes that get beginner resumes filtered out

        • One generic resume for every job. Each job post uses different words. Mirror the words of the specific post you're applying to — that's the whole game.
        • No Skills section. The ATS looks for a clear list of tools and skills. Burying "Excel" inside a paragraph is easy to miss; a Skills line is not.
        • Duties instead of results. "Responsible for answering emails" says what the job was. "Answered 60+ customer emails a day with a 98% satisfaction rating" says how well you did it — and adds keywords and numbers in one move.
        • Fancy formatting. Tables, text boxes, columns, headers/footers, and images can scramble your text when the ATS reads it. One column, standard headings, a normal font.
        • Missing contact details. No email or phone number on the resume itself means the recruiter can't reach you even if you pass the filter.
        • Wrong length. Under 200 words usually means missing content; over 1,000 usually means padding. For a beginner, one focused page — roughly 300–700 words — is the sweet spot.

        What score and grade do I need before applying?

        Aim for 75 or higher — grade B or better — against the specific job description you're applying to. Below 60 (grade D or F) usually means important keywords or a whole section is missing. The score is a guide, not a guarantee — a human still makes the final call.

        Should I copy the whole job description into my resume to get 100?

        No. Keyword stuffing is obvious to recruiters and can get you rejected at the human stage. Add the keywords you genuinely have, in sentences that describe real work you did.

        What file format should I send to employers?

        Follow the job post: if it asks for PDF, send PDF; if it doesn't say, a simple .docx or PDF from a one-column document both read well. Avoid Canva-style multi-column designs for ATS applications — they look great and parse badly. This checker reads pasted text and .txt files, so for PDF or Word resumes, copy the text out and paste it in.

        I have no job description yet. How should I use this?

        Pick your target role at the top of the tool. You'll be checked against the skills and keywords employers most commonly ask for in that role, so you can strengthen your resume before you start applying.
