Task Scam Explained: Like Videos, Then Pay to Unlock

A task scam starts with work that feels real, because at first you actually get paid. You are asked to like videos, rate products, write short reviews, or complete simple online tasks. Small payments arrive, which builds trust. Then the job changes: to unlock bigger tasks, higher pay, or a withdrawal, you must deposit your own money first. That deposit is the scam, and it is where the money disappears.

How it usually works

  1. You are contacted on WhatsApp, Telegram, or social media about an easy work-from-home job, often with no interview.
  2. Simple tasks pay small amounts, sometimes daily, so the job looks legitimate.
  3. You are moved to a group or a fake platform that shows a growing balance.
  4. To continue, withdraw, or reach a higher level, you must pay a deposit, fee, or top-up.
  5. Each payment leads to a larger payment request, until you stop paying or the account and the recruiter disappear.

The simple rule

A real job pays you for work. It never asks you to deposit money to unlock your own earnings, withdraw your pay, or qualify for bigger tasks. Any job that requires a deposit to continue is a task scam, no matter how much you were paid at the start.

Warning signs

  • Pay for liking, rating, or reviewing, with no real employer, contract, or careers page.
  • Work only through chat apps, with a recruiter who cannot be verified on an official company site.
  • A balance that grows on screen but can only be withdrawn after a payment.
  • Pressure to deposit quickly, or claims that a limited number of high-paying tasks will expire.
  • Requests to recruit other people, which turns the job into a chain that needs new deposits to keep going.

What to do if you are already in one

Stop depositing money, even if you have already paid and want to recover it. That urge to recover a loss is exactly how the scam grows. Save screenshots of the tasks, payments, messages, and the platform, and report the account and listing on the platform where you found it. If you shared bank details, contact your bank right away. In the USA, task scams can also be reported through the FTC fraud reporting site.
