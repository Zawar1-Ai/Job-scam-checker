          Before you apply — checklist

          Do these checks yourself, every time, no matter what the result above says:

          • Find the job on the company's official careers page — type the website address yourself instead of clicking a link you were sent.
          • Check the recruiter's email domain. A real recruiter usually emails from the company domain, not a free personal account.
          • Search the company name + "scam" (and + "reviews") and read what other people report.
          • Confirm there is a real interview or skills test before any offer. Real hiring takes steps.
          • Confirm there is no upfront payment of any kind — no training fee, deposit, or starter kit to buy.
          • Check the pay is in line with similar roles. If it sounds far too high for the work, treat it as a warning.
          • Share personal details only late in the process, and only through the company's official hiring system — never over chat.
          • Confirm the daily work is clear — what you will do each day, your hours, and who you report to.

          The 10 red flags this tool checks for

          These are the warning signs explained in our full guide, How to Spot Remote Job Scams. One flag does not always mean a scam — but every flag is a reason to slow down and verify.

          1. 1Upfront fee to startYou are asked to pay for training, equipment, software, or a starter kit before you earn anything.
          2. 2Fake cheque trickYou are sent a cheque to deposit, keep part of it, and send the rest onward. The cheque later bounces.
          3. 3Sensitive details asked too earlySocial Security number, bank login, full bank account, or a photo of your ID — before any real interview.
          4. 4Instant offer, no interviewYou are "hired" within hours, with no interview, no test, and no real questions about your skills.
          5. 5Unrealistic payPay far above normal for the role — for example, very high hourly pay for simple work with no experience.
          6. 6Recruiter only on chat appsEverything happens on WhatsApp or Telegram, or from a free personal email — no company domain, no careers page.
          7. 7Vague or copied description"Easy online work, earn from home" — with no clear daily duties, hours, or skills listed.
          8. 8Pressure to decide now"Accept today", "send the fee now to secure your spot", "limited seats" — real employers let you think.
          9. 9The work itself is the scamReshipping packages, moving money between accounts, cashing cheques for strangers, or posting fake reviews.
          10. 10Company cannot be verifiedNo official website or careers page, no traceable details — or a name almost identical to a well-known brand.

          Common questions

          Straight answers to the questions beginners ask most.

          Does a red flag prove the job is a scam?

          No. A flag is a warning sign, not proof. A small or new company might, for example, use a chat app once, or write a short job post. But some flags are never part of real hiring — a real employer does not ask you to pay a fee to start, deposit a cheque and forward money, or hand over your Social Security number before an interview.

          The more flags a post has, the more careful you should be. With 2 or more flags, our advice is simple: skip the listing and find the role through official channels instead.

          Can a real company hire without an interview?

          For almost all real jobs, no. Real hiring almost always includes at least one real conversation — a phone or video interview — and often a short skills test, so the company can check you can do the work. A company that offers you a job within hours, without speaking to you at all, has no way to know who you are. That is exactly why scammers skip the interview: it is faster, and it avoids questions.

          If you are ever unsure, ask for a video call and for the role to be confirmed from an official company email address. A real employer can do both.

          What if I already replied or sent details?

          Do not panic — but act now, in this order:

          1. Stop all contact. Do not send any money, and do not deposit any cheque you were sent.
          2. If you shared bank details or your Social Security number, contact your bank right away, tell them what happened, and ask them to watch or secure your account.
          3. Change the password on any account where you used the same password, and turn on two-factor sign-in.
          4. Save the messages and the job post, and report the listing on the platform where you found it.

          This page is general information, not legal or financial advice. If money was taken, also consider reporting it to your local police and, in the USA, at reportfraud.ftc.gov.

          Can I check just a link, without the job text?

          Yes — paste the link into the same box as the job text. There is only one box: the checker detects a link on its own (an address starting with http://, https://, or www., a single link on its own, or a link inside pasted text) and runs the link check. The link check looks at the address itself: the domain, whether it is a shortened link or a WhatsApp / Telegram link, suspicious words inside the address, whether it uses secure HTTPS, and whether the domain is a known job board or an unknown domain that only looks like one. If you paste the job text and a link together, both are checked and you get one combined result.

          The checker also tries to read the linked page so it can run the full text check. Many job sites block other websites from reading their pages — that is a normal security setting, not a sign of a scam by itself. When that happens, the checker tells you plainly and asks you to copy-paste the job text into the same box instead. A link verdict alone is never a guarantee: the text check is the stronger check, so use both when you can.

          Is the text I paste private?

          Yes. The check runs entirely in your browser, on your device. Nothing you paste is uploaded, stored, or shared anywhere — when you close this page, the text is gone. Please still avoid pasting your own passwords or bank details into any website, including this one; you never need them for a job check.
